When games inspire artists

Or sounds good for artists, besides video game developers and musicians creating the music, music artists like rappers or rockstars also find inspiration in the games that exist. Sometimes even borrow samples from game music or sound effects that fits the setting, causing it to become well known in a community or allowing future creators to take inspiration and use it for creativity.

Take a look at these examples. While you listen, learn the history behind how and why each artist created a song, borrowed a song, or used a sample to remix it.

If there are artists or musicians I Forgot use the tab or link there to leave a message, and I'll add it.

Sources: Songfacts and Billboard

01

M. Shadows - Not Ready to Die

Frontman M. Shadows explained in an interview that the song is about the Call of Duty Zombies story. He is a major fan of the game, so Treyarch sent him the full storyline and he made sure the song followed it. The song was made in four days and debuted exclusively in Call of Duty: Black Ops Zombies, where it became memorable to the Zombies community.

02

Zombie Nation - Kernkraft 400

If you've been to a professional hockey game, you may recognize the chant from Zombie Nation's career-defining hit. The melody actually samples "Stardust," a track from the 1984 computer game Lazy Jones. Zombie Nation heard the melody and reworked it, then paid the original game composer after the song reached the Billboard Hot 100 as thanks for allowing the melody to be used.

03

Drake - 6 God

Following the release of his February 2015 mixtape, fans noticed its sample of "Haunted Chase" from Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy's Kong Quest. Producers Boi-1da and Syk Sense heard the sample and built the beat around it. It is widely believed they grew up playing the SNES classic, whose atmospheric soundtrack became a favorite for hip-hop producers to flip.

04

DRAM - Cha Cha

DRAM's infectious hit "Cha Cha" prominently samples "Star Road" from Super Mario World. DRAM described the song as being about a good time, matching the upbeat and nostalgic feeling of the Mario world. After a legal dispute involving a similar-sounding Drake song, DRAM changed the track to sound closer to the original and avoid Nintendo trouble.

05

aespa - Hold on Tight

The film's producers wanted to modernize the iconic 8-bit theme "Korobeiniki" for a high-energy thriller. They commissioned aespa because its hyperpop and futuristic cyberpunk concept matched the movie's retro-tech aesthetic. The lyrics reflect the film's plot through game language about blocks, obstacles, and a twisted fantasy.
